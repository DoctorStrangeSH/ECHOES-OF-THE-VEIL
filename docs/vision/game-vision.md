# Game Vision — ECHOES OF THE VEIL

## Product promise

**Live an ordinary life in a city that is quietly becoming impossible — then decide what the truth is worth.**

ECHOES OF THE VEIL is a single-player, top-down narrative action RPG with life-simulation and light management systems. Its signature contrast is contemporary routine against an ancient, uncanny layer of reality. The player may pursue the central mystery, build a livelihood, form relationships, explore dangerous places, or combine all of these paths.

## Audience and positioning

Target players who enjoy:
- story-rich RPGs with mystery, exploration, and consequential choices;
- cozy or systemic life sims with shops, homes, crafting, and routines;
- top-down pixel-art worlds with secrets and meaningful environmental storytelling;
- action combat and long-term character progression.

The market position is **narrative mystery RPG + inhabitable modern town + supernatural exploration**, not an MMO and not a pure farming sim. Avoid feature-list competition with enormous open-world games. Win through a distinctive premise, strong writing, memorable characters, coherent systems, and a polished first 30 minutes.

## Creative pillars

1. **A compelling mystery.** Every major chapter answers one question, raises a sharper one, and leaves clues that make earlier scenes worth reinterpreting.
2. **People, not quest dispensers.** Named NPCs have needs, loyalties, relationships, secrets, and memories. Their simulation must support authored characterization rather than flatten everyone into generic schedules.
3. **A life beyond combat.** Investigation, work, business, crafting, exploration, relationships, and combat are valid ways to engage with the world.
4. **Consequences that can be understood.** Choices change relationships, access, local conditions, and later scenes. Avoid arbitrary hidden morality scores.
5. **Discovery with authored meaning.** Handcrafted story locations and bosses carry narrative weight; procedural systems provide variation around them, not replace them.
6. **A readable visual identity.** Everyday modern spaces feel warm and lived-in; Veil spaces are beautiful, uncanny, and increasingly unsettling.

## Player fantasy

The player begins as a newcomer with a customizable appearance and a modest place to stay. They are not secretly the chosen monarch or the only person capable of solving everything. Their unusual ability is to notice discrepancies between what the city remembers and what actually happened. The player earns influence through relationships, evidence, competence, and decisions.

## Core loop

Live in Greyford → hear about a problem → investigate / work / prepare → enter a Veil site or resolve a local conflict → bring back evidence, resources, or relationships → change the town and unlock new opportunities → uncover a deeper layer.

The loop must remain optional and flexible. A player who spends a session decorating a home or running a shop should still feel that the world is progressing.

## Scope strategy

Build one excellent district and its adjacent wild/Veil space before multiplying regions. The first public demo should prove:
- the city feels inhabited;
- the mystery hooks players quickly;
- movement, interaction, dialogue, and combat feel reliable;
- choices visibly matter;
- the art pipeline can produce coherent interiors and exteriors.

Do not promise 100–200 hours until content volume and retention are demonstrated. Set quality and completion targets first; playtime is an outcome, not a design goal.

## Non-goals for initial release

- MMO, always-online features, PvP, or live-service monetization.
- Huge seamless continent before streaming and content pipelines are proven.
- AI-generated dialogue at runtime as a dependency.
- Unbounded procedural quest generation.
- Multiple playable protagonists with separate campaigns.
- A fully simulated economy for every item and resident.

## Success measures

During development, measure:
- demo completion and where players stop;
- first-session return intent and qualitative feedback;
- clarity of goals and interaction discoverability;
- crash-free sessions, save reliability, and frame-time stability;
- whether players can describe the central mystery and the game's unique hook.

Commercial success cannot be guaranteed. The controllable strategy is to validate the hook early, build a strong demo, gather feedback, and invest in discoverability before launch.
