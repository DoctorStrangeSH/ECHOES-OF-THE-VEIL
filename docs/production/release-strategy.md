# Release & Audience Strategy

## Positioning

One-sentence store promise:

**A mysterious city. A life you can make your own. A past that refuses to stay buried.**

The hook should be demonstrated in footage, not explained only in long text: show a familiar modern street, a door opening into an impossible place, an NPC reacting to a changed memory, and the player returning to a town visibly affected by a choice.

## Audience validation before full production

1. Build a short prototype and test movement / interaction feel.
2. Create a 10–15 minute narrative teaser that ends on a strong mystery reveal.
3. Show the same build to players unfamiliar with the project.
4. Record comprehension, completion, curiosity, and friction points.
5. Revise the hook before scaling regions and systems.

Do not interpret likes or friendly comments as proof of market demand. Seek honest feedback from genre players and track whether they understand the differentiator.

## Steam preparation

Prepare only after a stable visual and gameplay identity exists:
- concise capsule art and readable logo;
- short gameplay trailer with real in-engine footage;
- screenshots showing town life, exploration, dialogue, and combat;
- clear feature list without promises for unimplemented systems;
- demo with a self-contained arc and an unresolved larger mystery;
- accurate language support, controller, and accessibility information;
- store page and wishlists well before launch, subject to platform requirements.

## Community and marketing

- Share regular, specific development updates: a character, a mystery clue, a location transformation, or a tested system.
- Use short clips that communicate the hook in seconds.
- Maintain a public roadmap with completed, in-progress, and planned labels.
- Invite playtesters to report confusion and bugs, not only vote on features.
- Build a press / creator kit with logo, screenshots, trailer, fact sheet, and contact.
- Avoid paid marketing spend until the demo's hook and store conversion signals justify it.

## Monetization

Recommended initial model: premium single-player game with a complete offline experience. Consider substantial expansions only after the base game is successful. Avoid pay-to-win, energy timers, loot boxes, or selling power. Cosmetic extras are optional and should not fragment the core experience.

## Commercial realism

Popularity and revenue cannot be guaranteed by scope or playtime. The most controllable factors are product quality, distinctiveness, a strong demo, discoverability, clear store messaging, localization, player trust, and disciplined scope. Budget for platform fees, taxes, refunds, localization, trailer/store assets, QA, and post-launch support before setting a price.
